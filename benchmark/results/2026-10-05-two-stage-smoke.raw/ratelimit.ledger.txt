OBSERVATIONS:
- Root cause (read from <repo>\benchmark\impl\ratelimit.py, confirmed by running it): `_attempts` is one module-global list and the `ip` argument is never read. The limiter is therefore global: 5 attempts per minute across ALL clients combined, not per IP. The six-distinct-IPs probe shows a brand-new sixth IP with zero prior attempts is blocked. The "per IP; different IPs are tracked independently" half of the request is not implemented, which is what criteria 5 and 6 test.
- Criterion 4 is PASS only in the narrow sense the criterion itself defines. The limiter takes no username, so it cannot split the count by username, and same-IP attempts do add up (6th blocked). That is a side effect of the global counter, not evidence of IP-keyed counting. Criteria 5 and 6 are where the lack of per-IP keying shows up.
- Mapping used for criteria 1 and 2: the module exposes only allow_login(ip) -> True/False. I read "processed normally" as True and "blocked by the limiter" as False. The credential-handling parts of criterion 1 (valid credentials log in, invalid ones fail) live outside this module and were not exercised; only the "not blocked by the limiter" part was. This is also why criterion 7 is NOT-EXERCISED.
- Window semantics observed: sliding window. An attempt stops counting exactly 60.00s after it was made (blocked at +59.99s, allowed at +60.00s). Blocked attempts are not recorded, so hammering while blocked does not extend the block. The request did not ask for either behaviour, so neither counts against it.
