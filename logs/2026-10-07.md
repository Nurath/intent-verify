and what was measured.

## A fourth review

## What the self-check changed

Round 1 of `/intent-verify` on the change ended as `MATCHES INTENT`, 15 of 15,
the evening before. Its observations named two things the verdict did not, and
fixing those turned up a third.

- **The observations are printed up to 6,000 characters** (`REPORT_OBSERVATIONS`
  in `tools/validate_ledger.py`). The limit shared with other notes, 800, cut
  the two ledgers kept from real sessions (939 and 4,536 characters); every
  benchmark ledger is under 530, which is how the limit looked fine. Commands
  and outputs keep their limits: 13% and 7% of the 222 stored values exceed
  them, and a cut one says how long it was.
- **A model without a score can be given a place** (`ranks_above` in the
  registry, read by `capability()` in `tools/select_verifier.py`). A complex
  change written by Opus went to Sonnet, because Fable 5.1 has no score and an
  unscored model ranked last. That had been seen the day before, and the test
  was changed to match the code; the question of what the chosen option
  promised was not asked. Fable 5.1 now stands just above Sonnet 5.5 and below
  Opus 5.5,
  where Fable 5 stood relative to its siblings in the August snapshot (59.9,
  against 53.4 and 60.7), and a warning says the pick rests on an assumption.
  The reading "top tier means T1" and the reading "top tier means the top
  model" are now both satisfied, so nothing had to be asked.
- **An unlisted candidate is no longer taken for another vendor's model.**
  Rewriting that test with a made-up model name showed that `--assume-tier`
  gave a candidate the family "unknown", that unknown counted as different
  from the implementer's, and that it was therefore preferred over every listed
  model, with the reason "different-family preference applied". Older than
  this release; it mattered less while the registry did not know the
  implementer either.
- **The skill** says what a value ending in `[cut: N characters in all]` is,
  tells the session to read the `OBSERVATIONS` line before writing the verdict
  and to say so beside it when a remark qualifies a `PASS`, and no longer says
  that capability above the floor "did not change a verdict on the measured
  sets" (one task was run on both models).

## A fifth review, and 0.5.1

The maintainer had 0.5.0 reviewed the day it was released, at `ceccfe2`. The
review confirmed the earlier findings as fixed, replayed the 19 new verifier
replies and the 77 ask-first manifests to the published numbers, and reported
two findings. Both reproduced.

| | Finding | Reproduced | Reached further |
|---|---|---|---|
| P2 | Scores from two versions of the index are ranked on one scale: `claude-opus-5` (60.7, old) beats `claude-opus-5-5` (58, new) for a complex change | yes | the gap to the implementer was subtracted across versions too, and Fable 5 outranked Fable 5.1 |
| P3 | A criterion's quote is printed with `repr()`: a 20,037-character line, 5,000 accents | yes | so was everything a `DEFECT:` line quotes from the reply |

The first is a rule the repository already had and 0.5.0 broke:
`docs/MODEL-COMPAT.md` said to refresh the snapshot in one commit and never
row by row, "or the tiers stop being comparable". The note on the new rows
even said to compare tiers and not scores. Nothing in the code did.

Shown the fixes and what attacking them found, the maintainer chose to ship
0.5.1 by PR, merged once all four CI checks are green.

### Changes shipped — 0.5.1

- `models/registry.json`: `scales`, newest first; the two rows read from the
  newer index carry `"scale": "v4.3.2"`.
- `tools/select_verifier.py`: candidates are ordered scored before unscored,
  then by tier, then by scale, then by score, so two numbers meet only within
  one version. A warning says when the newer version decided. The gap to the
  implementer is subtracted only within a version; otherwise it is "not
  measured" and `weak-verifier` goes by tiers, two or more apart.
- `tools/validate_ledger.py`: the displayed quote, every defect line, the ids
  an ambiguity names and the file names `--run` mentions go through the
  filter and a bound; at most fifty defect lines; the filter counts every
  mark, not only those with a combining class.

### Tests / verification

- 315 unit tests (290 at 0.5.0), hook selftest 35 of 35, mock benchmark exit
  0, all 180 recorded verdicts replay.
- The review's fixtures: the selector picks `claude-opus-5-5` and says why;
  the manifests print lines of 366 and 87 characters, and the written manifest
  keeps each quote whole.
- **Adversarial pass on the two fixes** (Sonnet 5.5; the fixes are by Opus
  5.5), before any commit. The fixes held on what they set out to do: 40,500
  generated manifests and ledgers gave the same exit code and written manifest
  as 0.5.0 with no crash, and 2.6 million selections never changed when one
  version's scores were shifted by a constant. Around them it found:
  - the filter capped marks by combining class, and 1,567 printable marks
    have class 0: piles of 795 went through, also in 0.5.0's own output;
  - one line still unbounded: the ids of an ambiguity, three million
    characters for one id repeated a million times;
  - a million defects were a million lines, and filtering each made that
    slower than before (6.3 s against 0.9 s);
  - the cut at 800 characters hid the one wrong number in a long numbering
    defect;
  - file names beside a run were printed raw, and a `run.json` nested too deep
    was a traceback;
  - putting the tier first let a candidate known only by `--assume-tier T1`
    outrank a measured T2 model, 75 picks of 328,860 that had nothing to do
    with two scales;
  - `weak-verifier` was still missing wherever one side has no score (Opus
    5.5 with Haiku 4.5), a score on an unlisted scale was compared while the
    warning said it was not, and two smaller untruths in warnings.
  All fixed, each with a test. Its probes and fuzzers were then run on the
  final code: piles of two, the ids line 142 characters, a million defects in
  0.3 s, 10,000 fresh fuzz cases with no difference from 0.5.0 in exit code or
  written manifest, 1.17 million shifted selections with no change of pick,
  1.08 million shuffled ones with no rule broken.
- **A mutation check went wrong and was caught by the suite.** To see the new
  tests fail, the tools were stashed and an older patch re-applied; the
  clean-up then checked out `models/` as well and lost the registry change.
  Nine tests failed at once, the change was re-applied, and the suite passed.
  The probes were re-run after that, not before.

- A defect line cut at 800 characters can lose the end of a criterion text

- Exact ties between candidates are still decided by the order listed.
- No model run was repeated: no prompt changed. The live check of 0.5.0 in
